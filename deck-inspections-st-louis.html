Deck safety inspection
Why this exists

A home inspection covers the whole house. Your deck gets a paragraph.

A general home inspector has an enormous amount of house to cover in a few hours: roof, foundation, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, appliances. The deck is one line item near the end, usually assessed by walking on it and giving the railing a shake. That is not a criticism of the trade, it is just the reality of the scope.

The trouble is that decks fail from underneath. The ledger connection to the house, the hardware holding it, the footings, the base of the posts where they meet the ground: none of that is visible from the deck surface, and all of it is where the serious problems start. Every year we get called out to repair decks that passed an inspection, sometimes recently.

The difference

We inspect decks because we repair decks. When we get underneath yours, we are looking at the same connections, hardware, and framing we replace every week, and we know what the early stage of each failure looks like.

The assessment

What a real deck inspection covers

We go over the deck top to bottom, including the parts you cannot see from the surface, and we assess the rest of the exterior woodwork while we are there.

The structure

  • Ledger attachment and flashing where the deck meets the house
  • Footings, and whether they have settled or shifted
  • Posts, including the base where they meet grade
  • Beams, joists, blocking, and span
  • Hangers, bolts, and fasteners for corrosion and pull-out
  • Overall load path, front to back

The surface and safety

  • Decking condition, rot, cupping, cracking, and soft spots
  • Railing height, strength, and post attachment
  • Baluster spacing and guard requirements
  • Stair stringers, treads, riser height, and tread depth
  • Landings, gates, and transitions
  • How it all measures against current code
Every exterior wood structure, not just the deck

The inspection covers the exterior woodwork across the whole property, because the conditions that take down a deck take down everything else built the same way. Posts sitting at grade, hardware giving up, and water shed the wrong way show up on all of it.

So along with the deck we assess porches and porch steps, pergolas, screened-in porches, covered patios, fences, and any stairs and landings on the property.

Good times to book one

When a deck safety inspection is worth it

Second opinion after a home inspection

Your inspector flagged the deck, or barely mentioned it, and you want to know what is actually going on under there.

Before you buy

A pre-purchase deck assessment tells you what you are inheriting, in time to do something about it at the negotiating table.

Before you list

Find out what a buyer’s inspector will find, on your schedule, with time to decide what to address first.

Your deck is over 15 years old

Standards for ledgers, hardware, and railings have moved. An older deck deserves a look against where the code is now.

Something changed

New bounce underfoot, a railing that moves, a corner sitting lower than it did last summer, or a hard winter behind you.

You are about to host on it

A deck full of people is the highest load it will ever carry. Worth knowing it is ready before the party, not after.

What you walk away with

A clear picture, in plain language

You get a straight assessment of where your deck stands: what is sound, what has started to go, and what needs attention now versus what you can plan for. We show you the actual conditions we found and explain what each one means for the deck as a whole.

If the inspection turns up work worth doing, we will tell you what it takes to fix, and we will also tell you when the answer is that your deck is in good shape. A deck inspection is a paid service and it stands on its own. Whether you hire us for the repair afterward is entirely up to you.

Keeping it clean

Inspections are billed as their own service, at one flat rate that covers the whole property. Repair and rehab estimates are separate, and those are always free.

If the assessment does turn up problems, most of them are fixable without replacing the deck. Boards, railings, and stairs are routine deck repair. Even a failing beam or support is usually a targeted structural repair rather than a rebuild, and when several things have gone at once, a deck rehab restores the whole deck for 25 to 60% less than replacing it.
