Deck rehab & restoration
The idea

What a deck rehab actually is

A deck is really two things stacked on top of each other. Underneath there is the structure: footings, posts, beams, joists, and the connection to the house. On top there is everything you see and touch: the decking, railings, stairs, and trim. The weather gets to the visible half first, which is why a deck can look completely finished while the half doing the actual work is still in good shape.

A deck rehab treats those two halves separately. We assess the structure honestly, repair or reinforce whatever in it has failed, and then replace the visible material on top. What you get back is a deck that is new where it counts and original where original is still strong. Same footprint, same layout, and as good as a new build, because everything that would not have lasted another twenty years has been replaced.

The short version

A replacement pays to throw away the parts of your deck that are still working. A rehab pays only for the parts that stopped.

Rehab vs. replace

How much a deck rehab saves versus replacing

Two numbers come up on our estimates over and over, and both of them stay in your pocket.

When the bones are good, rehabbing an existing deck runs 25 to 60% less than tearing it out and building new. And when the trouble comes down to one failing beam, post, or footing, a targeted structural repair can land up to 75% under the cost of a whole new deck.

The difference is easy to trace. A replacement pays for demolition, disposal, new footings, new framing, new everything, and weeks of crew time in your yard. A rehab skips most of that, because most of that is already behind your house and still doing its job. You are paying for the material that failed and the skill to swap it out cleanly, and that is a much shorter list.

Is your deck a candidate?

When a deck rehab is the right call

A rehab is on the table whenever the structure is still carrying its load. In practice that usually looks like this:

  • The footings and posts are stable and set correctly
  • The framing is sound, or the failures are limited and reachable
  • The problems are in the decking, railings, and stairs
  • The deck attaches to the house properly, or can be corrected
  • The layout still works for how you use the yard

Hit most of those and a rehab will almost always be the better outcome, on cost and on time.

Our rule

When we will tell you to replace instead

Here is the standard we hold ourselves to: we will not rehab a deck that would not last as long as a brand new one. If yours genuinely needs to be replaced, we will say so plainly at the estimate, with the reasons in front of you.

The vast majority of the time, it does not. Most decks have good bones and just need the right work to bring them back to solid, safe, and good for decades. Three things decide how long that lasts: the materials, the location, and how well you keep up with it. Keep up with it and you are looking at 20 years.

The work itself

What we keep, and what we replace

Every rehab is scoped from what we find on the deck, but the split usually falls out like this.

Usually kept

  • Footings that are stable and correctly set
  • Posts and beams still carrying their load
  • Sound joists and blocking
  • A ledger that is properly attached and flashed
  • The footprint, layout, and elevation you already like

Usually replaced

  • Decking that has rotted, cracked, cupped, or splintered
  • Railings, posts, and balusters, brought to current code
  • Stair treads, stringers, and landings
  • Individual joists and hangers that have failed
  • Fasteners and hardware that have corroded
Why we can save decks others cannot

The structural work that makes a rehab possible

A rehab is only honest if somebody actually gets under the deck and is willing to fix what they find. Replacing a beam or a rotted support post is highly technical work, and it is genuinely dangerous. It can mean carrying the full weight of the deck on temporary supports and working underneath it while you do. That is exactly why a lot of companies skip past it and recommend a replacement instead.

We take that work on. It is the reason we can look at a deck another company wrote off and tell you it is worth saving, and it is where the biggest savings on this page come from.
